🫚 GingerBooks: owner guide

GingerBooks runs your ginger candy business: billing, stock, production, payments, expenses, GST reports and profit. It is a single file and needs no installation or internet connection. It costs nothing to run.

2. Where your data is kept (please read)

Your entries are saved inside the browser on this laptop, not inside the HTML file. Use the same browser every time. Clearing browsing data, resetting Windows or changing laptops will lose the data unless you have a backup.

Set up automatic backup once (2 minutes): Settings & backup → Choose auto-backup file → open C:\GingerCan\Backups → Save. Every change is then copied to that file automatically. If Chrome later asks for permission again, click Reconnect on the Alerts page.

For a cloud copy at no cost: put the Backups folder inside OneDrive or Google Drive, or click Download backup now once a week and email the file to yourself.

New laptop: copy GingerBooks.html across, open it, then go to Settings → Restore from backup and choose the latest .json backup file.

Cloud sync: same books on laptop, website and phone

The laptop file and the website https://vpprasanth.github.io/gingerbooks/ can share one set of books through the cloud.

  1. Open GingerBooks (laptop file or website) → Settings & backup → Cloud sync.
  2. Type your email and password (Prasanth gives you these). Click Sign in and sync.
  3. Do the same on every other device. The top bar shows Synced when all is up to date.

Each entry is saved on the device first. It goes to the cloud within a few seconds when there is internet, and other devices pick it up within 30 seconds. With no internet the app keeps working and sends the entries later.

First sign-in: sign in first on the device that already has your entries (the laptop). Its books go to the cloud. The other devices then download them.

If two devices changed the books at the same time, the app asks which copy to keep. It saves a backup file of this device's copy before it uses the cloud copy, so nothing is lost. Keep the auto-backup file switched on as well.

Users and login

Settings & backup → Users and login. Create the administrator first (user name and password). The administrator then adds each user with a user name, name, email and role:

  • Administrator can do everything, including managing users.
  • Staff can do everything except open Settings & backup.

Adding a user, or clicking Reset password, makes a temporary password. It is shown once, with an ✉ Email button that opens your mail app with the user name, password and website link ready to send. When the user logs in, they must choose their own password. Anyone can change their user name and password later under 👤 My account; the administrator can also rename any user in the Users table at the top of the menu. If someone forgets theirs, the administrator resets it. Disable or delete users who leave.

Each laptop or phone asks once and then opens straight away until someone clicks Log out. Untick Remember this device on a shared computer. On the website version you can also turn on fingerprint / face unlock. If the administrator forgets their password, click Administrator: unlock with cloud account on the login screen.

Attachments

Every invoice, purchase, order, payment, expense, return, production run, product, customer, supplier, vendor and worker has a 📎 button. Use it to attach bills, cheque / DD scans, delivery notes, PDFs and emails (save the email as .eml or PDF, or paste its text as a note), or to take a photo with the phone. Photos are shrunk automatically and each file can be up to 10 MB. With cloud sync on, files are kept in the cloud, so the phone and laptop both see them. Without sync they stay on that device only and are not in the backup file.

3. First-time setup (in this order)

  1. Try it first (optional): Settings → Load sample data and click through every page. Then click Erase all data (type ERASE) before you enter real data.
  2. Settings: business name, GSTIN, state, address, FSSAI number, bank/UPI details, invoice terms and monthly sales target.
  3. Products: add each candy pack (type Finished), each raw material (ginger, jaggery, sugar: type Raw material) and each packing item you keep in stock (pouches, jars, labels: type Packing). Enter the HSN code (sugar confectionery is usually 1704), GST rate, selling price, cost, reorder level and shelf life in days. Confirm the GST rate with your accountant.
  4. Customers, Suppliers, Vendors:
    • Customers are the people who buy from you. Enter the GSTIN if they have one, plus their default discount % and credit days.
    • Suppliers sell you goods: ginger, jaggery, pouches. Also enter their delivery lead time.
    • Vendors provide services: transporter, electricity board, dryer service technician, label printer, ad agency, auditor.
  5. Opening stock: Stock & batches → + Opening stock. Add each batch you have today with its expiry date.
  6. Backup: set up the auto-backup file (section 2).

4. Daily work

You sellSales invoices → + New invoice. Pick the customer; the place of supply decides CGST+SGST or IGST automatically. Choose products. The batch is picked automatically, earliest expiry first. Set the price in either of two ways: type the rate and discount %, or type the margin % you want over your cost and the rate is calculated for you. “Same discount / margin on all lines” applies one figure to every line. Goods arrive from a supplierPurchases → + New purchase. Enter the supplier bill number, batch, expiry, quantity and rate.
You cook / pack a batchProduction → + Record production. Enter the quantity made and the materials used. The new batch gets an expiry date automatically, and its cost is worked out from the materials.
Money comes in later (UPI, cheque, DD, cash, NEFT)Payments & dues → + Record payment. The amount is adjusted against the oldest unpaid invoices. Cheques and DDs stay Awaiting clearance until the bank statement shows them, or until you click Cleared / Bounced.
You pay an expenseExpenses → + Add expense. Choose the type: OpEx for running costs, CapEx for machines and assets, or Not a cost for supplier payments, GST paid and loans. Then choose the category and the vendor. Use Repeat to copy last month's rent or salary entry.

You return goods to a supplierReturns → Return to supplier. This makes a debit note.

5. Cost heads and expense categories

Every expense category belongs to one cost head. Profit & break-even → Total cost per unit adds them up and divides by the quantity made in production runs.

Procurement: raw ginger (if not bought through Purchases), freight for raw material, loading, mandi commission, sorting and grading.
Processing: washing and cleaning, peeling/grating job work, fuel/gas/firewood, baking/cooking charges, drying charges, electricity, water, consumables, dryer / machine service charges (AMC), machine repairs.
Labour: daily/piece-rate labour, packing labour, employee salaries, staff welfare, PF/ESI, bonus.
Ingredients: lemon/lime, salt, sugar/jaggery, honey, spices and flavours.
Packaging: labels & stickers, printing, bottles/jars/caps, pouches, bottling/filling charges, cartons and tape, barcode/batch coding.
Advertising & marketing: ads, social media, banners, exhibitions, website, free samples.
Travel: travel & conveyance, vehicle fuel, vehicle maintenance, food and stay on trips.
Distribution & delivery: freight to customers, courier, hamali, distributor commission, marketplace fees.
Financial & audit: auditor/accountant, legal fees, bank charges, payment gateway charges, loan interest, insurance, GST late fees, software.
Premises & office: rent, storage/warehouse, security, cleaning & pest control, building repairs, phone/internet, stationery.
Compliance & quality: FSSAI and trade licences, lab testing, trademark.
Wastage & losses: process wastage, damaged goods. Stock written off in Stock is added automatically.
Miscellaneous: anything else.
Depreciation: worked out automatically from CapEx (machinery, dryer, vessels, sealing machine, scales, vehicle, building).

Recording a production run: enter the materials and quantities used, tick the workers who worked on the run (only workers on the rolls that day are listed; their wage is filled in and can be changed), then enter electricity units, fuel and any other cost. The app shows the lot cost and the cost per unit before you save. Leave Also record daily wages, fuel and other costs as cash expenses ticked so they appear in Expenses and Profit. Monthly-salaried staff are costed at salary ÷ 26 per run, and their salary stays a monthly expense. The monthly electricity bill is still entered under Expenses.

Workers (menu → Workers) is the labour register: name, gender, date of birth (age is worked out), phone, work, pay per day / month / piece, incentive, joining and leaving dates, pay-to account, emergency contact and remarks.

Daily production cost (Production page, and the last 7 days on the Dashboard): for each day it shows what was made, the materials used, that day's direct making costs (labour, fuel, washing, drying…), a fair share of the month's fixed making costs (salaries, electricity, machine AMC, depreciation), the cost per unit and the day's average selling price. Click a date to see the full breakdown by cost head. Enter daily wages and fuel on the day they are paid, marked Variable, so each day's cost is right.

Every number and box on the Dashboard is clickable, and each section has a Details › link to the full page.

Ginger, lemon, salt, sugar, bottles and labels bought in bulk are best added as products (type Raw material or Packing) and bought through Purchases. Production then deducts them, and their cost lands under the right head. In Products you can set each item's Cost head; by default anything named ginger goes to Procurement, other raw materials to Ingredients and packing items to Packaging.

You can type your own category name if none of these fit. Mark each expense Fixed (same every month, e.g. rent and salaries) or Variable (rises with sales, e.g. labels and transport). The break-even figure depends on this split.

Labels, pouches and jars that you buy in bulk and want to count are better added as Packing products and bought through Purchases. The app then deducts them in Production and includes them in product cost.

6. Bank statement

  1. In net banking, download the statement as Excel (.xlsx) or CSV. Save it in C:\GingerCan\Bank statements. If the bank gives an old .xls file, open it in Excel and use Save As → Excel Workbook (.xlsx).
  2. Bank statement → Import statement. Check that the columns are mapped correctly, then click Import.
  3. Money in is matched to the UPI, cheque and NEFT payments you recorded. Where there is no match, the app suggests an invoice of the same amount, or a customer whose name appears in the narration.
  4. For money out, click Save as expense. The category is guessed from the narration (TNEB → Electricity, DTDC → Courier, and so on).

Linking directly to the bank account is not possible in a free tool. In India that requires RBI's licensed Account Aggregator network. Importing the statement takes about a minute a week. Importing the same file twice is safe because duplicates are skipped.

7. Reading the reports

  • Dashboard: today's and this month's sales; a period table (today, week, month, quarter, year) with like-for-like comparisons; target progress; payment mix; trend chart; fast movers with 🔥 spikes; top suppliers and customers; returns.
  • Alerts: negative stock, empty (zero) batches, expired and expiring stock, low stock, items sold below cost, dead stock, overdue payments, cheques not cleared, overdue orders, backup due.
  • Demand vs supply: pending orders against good stock, and how much to make or buy now.
  • GST & reports: pick the month. You get GSTR-1 tables (B2B, B2C large and small, credit notes, HSN summary, documents issued) and a GSTR-3B summary (output tax, input credit, cash payable). There are also sales, purchase and returns registers and stock/expiry reports. Every table has an Excel (CSV) button. Give these to your accountant; they are working summaries, so check them against the GST portal before filing.
  • Profit & break-even: a profit & loss statement; the break-even sales per month and your margin of safety; contribution by product; and a what-if calculator (price, cost per unit, fixed cost) with a chart.
  • Party analysis: customers grouped by activity (Active, Slowing, At risk, Lost), share of sales, margin, returns, dues; suppliers by share, return %, and raw-material price movement; vendors by spending.

8. Rules the app follows

  • Invoice numbers run per financial year, for example INV/2026-27/0001. A saved invoice cannot be edited. Cancel it and make a new one. The cancelled number is still reported in GSTR-1 “documents issued”, as GST rules require.
  • If you bill more than you have in stock, the app warns you but lets you save. The item then shows in Alerts as negative stock until you enter the missing purchase or production.
  • Expired batches are never picked automatically for sale.
