Getting started

GingerBooks runs the ginger candy business: billing, stock, production, payments, expenses, payroll, GST reports and profit. It is one file, works without internet, and costs nothing to run.

Folder / fileWhat it is for
GingerBooks.htmlThe app. Double-click to open it in Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge. If it opens in another browser, right-click → Open with → Chrome.
BackupsSafety copies of all data. Keep the auto-backup file and yearly archive files here.
Bank statementsStatements downloaded from net banking, before importing them.
InvoicesInvoice and credit note PDFs.
ReportsGST reports, the P&L and other Excel (CSV) downloads.
GuideThis guide.
Right-click GingerBooks.html → Send to → Desktop (create shortcut) to open it from the desktop.
Entries are saved inside the browser on this laptop, not inside the HTML file. Use the same browser every time. Clearing browsing data, resetting Windows or changing laptops loses the data unless there is a backup or cloud sync.

Automatic backup (2 minutes, once): Settings & backup → Choose auto-backup file → open C:\GingerCan\Backups → Save. Every change is then copied to that file. If Chrome later asks for permission again, click Reconnect on the Alerts page.

Extra copy at no cost: keep the Backups folder inside OneDrive or Google Drive, or click Download backup now once a week and email the file to yourself.

New laptop: copy GingerBooks.html across, open it, then Settings → Restore from backup and choose the latest .json file. With cloud sync, signing in is enough.

The laptop file and the website vpprasanth.github.io/gingerbooks share one set of books through a free cloud account.

  1. Open GingerBooks → Settings & backup → Cloud sync.
  2. Type the business email and password, then Sign in and sync.
  3. Repeat on every other device. The top bar shows Synced when all is up to date.

Each entry is saved on the device first and reaches the cloud within seconds when there is internet. Other devices pick it up within 30 seconds. Without internet the app keeps working and sends entries later.

First sign-in: sign in first on the device that already has the entries (the laptop). Its books go to the cloud; other devices then download them.

If two devices changed the books at the same moment, the app asks which copy to keep, and saves a backup of this device's copy before replacing anything.

Open the website on the phone. The menu sits behind ☰; the bar at the bottom jumps to Home, Sales, Production and Payments.

  • Android (Chrome): ☰ → Install app on this device, or Chrome ⋮ → Add to Home screen.
  • iPhone (Safari): Share → Add to Home Screen.

The GingerBooks icon then opens full-screen and still opens without internet. On a new phone, sign in once under Settings → Cloud sync.

Settings & backup → Users and login. Create the Administrator first. The Administrator then adds each person with a user name, name, email and role.

RoleCan do
AdministratorEverything, including users, settings, erase and archive.
StaffEverything except Settings & backup and Payroll.
GuestView only, for a fixed number of days (see below).

Adding a user or pressing Reset password creates a temporary password, shown once, with an ✉ Email button. At first login the person chooses their own password. Anyone can change their user name and password under 👤 My account. Disable or delete users who leave.

Each device asks once and then opens straight away until Log out. Untick Remember this device on a shared computer. The website version also offers fingerprint or face unlock.

Settings & backup → Guests (needs cloud sync). Enter the guest's name, email, number of days (30 by default) and, if wanted, one financial year. Press Create guest and password, then ✉ Email to guest.

A guest sees every page, opens attachments, prints and downloads reports. A guest cannot add, change or delete anything, cannot open Settings, and does not see workers' personal or bank details. Access ends on the end date because the cloud checks it, so changing the computer clock does not help. Use +30 days, End now, Reset password or Delete at any time.

The plain website link is safe to share. Without a login a visitor sees only an empty app.
  1. Try it first (optional): Settings → Load sample data and click through every page, then start fresh.
  2. Settings: business name, GSTIN, state, address, FSSAI number, bank/UPI details, invoice terms, invoice prefix and monthly sales target.
  3. Products: each candy pack (type Finished), each raw material (Raw material), packing (Packing) and stationery or consumables you want to count. Fill in HSN, GST rate, selling price, reorder level and shelf life (what each field means).
  4. Customers, Suppliers, Vendors: customers buy from you (GSTIN, default discount, credit days). Suppliers sell you goods; list the goods each one supplies (how). Vendors provide services such as transport, electricity, repairs and audit.
  5. Workers: joining date and pay history.
  6. Opening stock: Stock & batches → + Opening stock, each batch with its cost and expiry date.
  7. Backup: set up the auto-backup file, and cloud sync if you use the phone.

Settings → Try it out / start fresh has two buttons. Both are for the Administrator and both download a backup first.

ButtonRemovesKeeps
Erase entries only (keep setup)
type START FRESH		Every entry: invoices, purchases, production, stock, payments, expenses, payroll, attachments, change log. Invoice numbers restart at 0001.Products, customers, suppliers, vendors, workers, users, guests, settings.
Erase everything
type ERASE		All of the above and the setup.Nothing. The app is like new.

Both clear the cloud copy too when sync is on. Afterwards enter the real opening stock and any old bills of this year, and refresh other devices.

Sales

Invoices, orders, payments received and customer returns.

  1. Sales invoices → + New invoice. Pick the customer. Their discount and the place of supply fill in.
  2. The place of supply decides the tax: same state as the business means CGST + SGST, another state means IGST.
  3. Choose products. The batch is picked automatically, earliest expiry first, and expired batches are never picked.
  4. Set the price: type the rate before GST, or the rate with GST under it, or the markup % you want over cost. Add a discount % if any (the GST rate sits just below it). Or type the Taxable value you want for the line, and the discount % (2 decimals) is worked out for you; any small gap from your figure is shown under the box.
  5. Choose how payment was received, then Save & print. In the print window choose Save as PDF and save it in C:\GingerCan\Invoices.
"Same discount on all lines" and "Same markup on all lines" apply one figure to every line at once.

How it is worked out: invoice line and GST · round-off · CGST/SGST or IGST · markup

Under each rate the invoice shows cost …. This is the real material cost of the good batches in stock, from production runs and purchases. It is not the fallback cost typed in Products. The box below the lines shows two figures:

  • Markup on cost = profit ÷ cost. Rate 66.66 and cost 41.26 give 61.6%.
  • Margin on sale = profit ÷ sale value. The same line gives 38.1%.

Changing the price in Products affects only new lines; the markup always follows the rate typed on the invoice.

How it is worked out: markup and margin · current cost

An invoice number has three parts: a prefix (letters), the serial and the financial year. In Settings → Invoice numbers choose the order of the parts, for example A207/2026-27 (prefix and serial, then year) or A/2026-27/0207, or type your own pattern. You can also set the prefix and the next number, for example to continue from the last paper bill. The serial starts again at 1 on 1 April by itself.

GST rule: up to 16 characters, using only letters, numbers, hyphen (-) and slash (/). Spaces are not allowed, so "A 207 / 2026-27" is written A207/2026-27. The app warns if a number breaks this rule.

How it is worked out: invoice numbers and the financial year

Old bills: on a new invoice tick Old bill already issued, type the printed number and date. Choose Do not change stock if that stock was never entered. If the old number is ahead of the counter, new invoices continue after it.

Orders → + New order with the due date. When you deliver, press Make invoice; pending quantities fill in. Part deliveries are tracked, and an order past its due date shows in Alerts.

Payments & dues → + Record payment. Choose the mode (cash, UPI, NEFT, cheque, DD) and reference. The amount is set against the customer's oldest unpaid invoices first. Cheques and DDs stay Awaiting clearance until the bank statement shows them or you press Cleared / Bounced.

An invoice is overdue when its age passes the customer's credit days (30 if not set). Overdue bills and cheques not cleared after 4 days appear in Alerts.

How it is worked out: payment allocation and overdue

Returns → Customer return. Choose the invoice and quantities. This makes a credit note with the same GST split as the invoice. Untick put back into stock if the goods are damaged; they are then not added back.

  • A saved invoice cannot be edited. Cancel it (with a reason) and make a new one. The cancelled number is still reported in GSTR-1 "documents issued", as GST rules require.
  • If you bill more than is in stock, the app warns but lets you save. The item shows as negative stock in Alerts until the missing purchase or production is entered.
  • Expired batches are never picked for sale.

Purchases & stock

Products and their types, suppliers and the goods they supply, purchases, stock, batches and reorder planning.

TypeUsed forHow its cost is counted
FinishedCandy packs you sellBatch cost from production runs; becomes cost of goods sold when sold.
Raw materialGinger, jaggery, sugar, lemon…Direct material. Picked in production runs; goes into batch cost.
PackingPouches, jars, labels, cartonsDirect material, like raw material.
Consumables / stationeryPaper, bill books, gloves, cleaning liquidOverhead. Kept in stock; when used up (record it here), counted in Profit & loss as overhead.
Other, or your own typesSpare parts, cleaning supplies…Overhead, like consumables.

An Administrator adds a new type by choosing ＋ Add a new type… in the Type box. To make a consumable usable in production (gloves, gas), set its Cost head to a production head such as Processing. Setting Overhead keeps a raw material out of production.

FieldMeaning
Selling price (before / with GST)Type either one; the other fills itself from the GST rate.
Purchase / making cost (fallback)Used only when the app has no real cost yet. Real cost comes from production runs and purchase bills.
Reorder levelWhen stock falls to this quantity, a Low stock alert appears. Blank means no alert.
Shelf life (days)Expiry date of a new batch = date made + shelf life, unless typed.
Lead time (days)Days needed to make or buy it; used by Demand vs supply.
Cost headWhere the item's cost goes: Procurement, Ingredients, Packaging, Processing, or Overhead. Auto: ginger → Procurement, other raw materials → Ingredients, packing → Packaging, other types → overhead.

Suppliers → Edit → Goods this supplier supplies. Add, change or remove items with the usual price. A new item can be created right there. These are the same items as in Products, so stock and costing stay in one place.

Each saved purchase updates the supplier's usual price to the latest rate and adds any new item to their list.

Price history: the same page lists, for each material, every rate paid to this supplier by date, newest first, with ▲ for a rise and ▼ for a fall, plus the lowest, highest and overall change. Products → Edit shows purchase prices from all suppliers and, for finished goods, the selling rates on invoices. Prices can change any time: each bill keeps its own rate, and past bills never change.

  1. Purchases → + New purchase. Choose the supplier first.
  2. The item list shows that supplier's goods first (★), with the usual price filled in, then other materials grouped by type. Choose All products to buy finished goods for resale.
  3. Enter the supplier bill number and date, batch, expiry, quantity and rate before GST.
  4. Save. Stock goes up as a new batch at the purchase rate. GST on the bill is input tax credit, not cost.

Each purchase, production run and opening stock entry creates a batch with its own cost and expiry. Stock & batches lists every item with its batches, quantity left, cost per unit and status: Good, Expiring soon (within 30 days by default), Expired, Empty or Negative.

  • Close an empty batch to hide it.
  • Write off an expired batch; its value goes to Wastage in Profit & loss.

Stock → Stock correction / wastage. Choose the item, batch, quantity and reason:

ReasonWhere the value goes
Wastage / damage, Expired, Samples, Count difference, OtherStock written off / wastage line in Profit & loss
Used up (office / overhead)Overhead line "Consumables & stationery used" in Profit & loss (Premises & office head)
The reason is chosen for you: consumables and other overhead items default to Used up, production materials to Wastage.

How it is worked out: value of consumables used

For each finished product: pending orders, good stock, shortfall, average sales per day, days the stock lasts, and a suggested quantity to make or buy now. For materials: stock, use in the last 30 days, days it lasts, and the reorder level. See how the suggestion is worked out and how long stock lasts.

Production & costing

Recording cooking and packing runs, and understanding what each unit costs.

  1. Production → + Record production. Choose the product and quantity made. Batch number and expiry fill in.
  2. Add the materials and quantities used. Only direct materials are listed (raw material, packing, or items with a production cost head). They are taken from the earliest-expiring batches.
  3. Tick the workers on the run. Only workers on the rolls that day are listed; their wage fills in and can be changed. Monthly staff are costed at salary ÷ 26 for the run.
  4. Enter electricity units, fuel/gas and any other cost.
  5. Check the lot cost and cost per unit shown before saving. Leave Also record daily wages, fuel and other costs as cash expenses ticked so they appear in Expenses and Profit.
The monthly electricity bill is still entered under Expenses. If runs record electricity, the bill is not added again to the daily overhead.

On the Production page (and the last 7 days on the Dashboard), each day shows what was made, the materials used, that day's direct making costs, a fair share of the month's fixed making costs, the cost per unit and the day's average selling price. Tap a date for the breakdown by cost head.

How it is worked out: daily cost and overhead share · depreciation

Production → Cost analysis shows, for each product, the cost per unit made split into materials, labour, power, fuel and other; a 6-month trend with ▲ marking rises; and each material's quantity per unit, cost, share and last purchase price.

How it is worked out: batch and lot cost · which batch is used first

Production → What-if prices. Type a new price for any material, or a % change for labour and electricity. The table shows the new cost per unit, the new profit per unit, and the selling price that keeps today's margin %. Nothing is saved.

How it is worked out: what-if prices

  • Making cost per unit in the last 30 days is more than 10% above the 3 months before.
  • Making cost has reached the selling price.
  • A material was bought (in the last 60 days) more than 10% above its earlier average.

The 10% can be changed at the bottom of What-if prices. Exact rules.

Every cost belongs to one head. Heads are grouped as cost to make, cost to sell, and cost to run the business.

HeadCovers
ProcurementRaw ginger and bringing it in: freight, loading, mandi commission, sorting.
ProcessingWashing, grating, cooking, drying, power, fuel, water, machine upkeep.
LabourWages, salaries, staff welfare, PF/ESI, bonus.
IngredientsLemon, salt, sugar, jaggery, honey, spices.
PackagingLabels, printing, bottles, jars, pouches, cartons, batch coding.
Wastage & lossesProcess wastage, damaged goods, stock written off.
DepreciationCapEx spread over its life (worked out automatically).
Advertising & marketing / Travel / Distribution & deliveryCost to sell.
Financial & audit / Premises & office / Compliance & quality / MiscellaneousCost to run the business. Consumables used up from stock go to Premises & office.

Expenses

Running costs, assets, recurring bills and the bank statement.

KindUse forEffect
OpExRunning costs: rent, electricity, transport, salariesReduces profit in the month paid.
CapExMachines and assets: dryer, sealing machine, vehicleSpread over its life as depreciation.
Not a costSupplier payments, GST paid, loan repayment, drawingsRecorded for cash, never in profit.

Mark each OpEx Fixed (same every month, like rent) or Variable (rises with sales or production, like labels or transport). Break-even depends on this split. Unknown categories can be typed in.

See depreciation and cost heads.

When adding an expense choose Repeats: every day, month, 2, 3 or 6 months, or year, and the last date. Say whether the amount is for each bill or the total for the whole period, split equally. The app shows the number of entries before saving.

How it is worked out: recurring dates and splitting

Example
Electricity ₹6,000 every 2 months from 15-01-2025 until 31-12-2025 → 6 entries (Jan, Mar, May, Jul, Sep, Nov).
Rent ₹1,20,000 total for 05-04-2025 to 31-03-2026, every month → 12 entries of ₹10,000.
  1. Download the statement from net banking as Excel (.xlsx) or CSV into C:\GingerCan\Bank statements.
  2. Bank statement → Import statement. Check the column mapping, then Import.
  3. Money in is matched to recorded UPI, cheque and NEFT payments, or to an invoice of the same amount, or to a customer named in the narration.
  4. For money out, press Save as expense; the category is guessed from the narration.
Importing the same file twice is safe: duplicates are skipped. A direct bank link is not possible in a free tool (it needs RBI's Account Aggregator network).

Every entry has a 📎 button for bills, cheque scans, delivery notes, PDFs and emails, or a phone photo. Photos are shrunk before upload, like WhatsApp does, and files can be up to 10 MB. With cloud sync, files are kept in the cloud so every device sees them.

Payroll

Workers, pay history, monthly pay, past salaries and corrections.

Workers → Edit: name, phone, work, pay type (per day, per month, per piece), incentive, joining and leaving dates, pay-to account, emergency contact. Pay history has one line per pay change with its start date. If you just change the wage later, the app asks from which date it applies and adds the line.

  • Monthly staff: salary in force on each day, for the days on the rolls. Joining on the 16th gives about half a month.
  • Daily workers: the amounts ticked for them in that month's production runs. Days outside runs are added with Adjust.
  • Adjust also adds incentive, bonus or a deduction (advance recovered, loss of pay), with a note.

Press Paid for one worker, or Mark all as paid. Pay then goes to Expenses. Wages already booked from a production run are not added again. If the month also has salary expenses typed by hand, a warning appears.

How it is worked out: payroll calculations · monthly staff in runs

Press Fill in past salaries and choose the months. Draft pay entries are made for every worker from their joining date. For daily workers in months without production records, the number of days you give (26 by default) is used. Check the drafts in Year summary, then Mark all drafts as paid. Nothing reaches Expenses before that.

Expenses, payments received, production notes and paid payroll months can be edited; each edit asks for a reason. Invoices, purchases and stock are corrected by cancelling and entering again, because GST returns depend on them.

The Change log lists every edit, cancellation, salary payment and back-dated entry with who, when, old → new and the reason. It cannot be changed or deleted. Guests can see and download it. Payroll is visible to Administrators and guests, not to Staff.

Reports

What each report shows and how to read it.

Today's and this month's sales, a period table (today, week, month, quarter, financial year) with comparisons, target progress, payment mix, 30-day trend, fast movers with 🔥 spikes, slow movers, top suppliers and customers, returns, dues and the estimated GST payable. Every number is clickable.

How the math works

Every calculation GingerBooks makes, with the formula and a worked example in rupees. Where it helps, a small calculator lets you try your own numbers. The examples read fully without the calculators.

Every money figure is rounded to 2 decimals (paise), half up. The invoice total alone is rounded to the nearest rupee, and the difference is printed as Round off.

Example
66.665 → 66.67 · 1520.076 → 1520.08 · invoice 2,593.58 → ₹2,594, round off +0.42.
Price before GST = Price with GST ÷ (1 + GST% ÷ 100)
Price with GST = Price before GST × (1 + GST% ÷ 100)
Example
₹70 with 5% GST → 70 ÷ 1.05 = ₹66.67 before GST.
Back again: 66.67 × 1.05 = 70.0035 → ₹70.00.
🧮 Try it: convert a price
Taxable value = Qty × Rate × (1 − Discount% ÷ 100)
GST = Taxable value × GST%
Same state: CGST = GST ÷ 2, SGST = GST − CGST (any odd paisa goes to SGST)
Other state: IGST = GST
Line total = Taxable value + GST
Rate and discount are kept to 2 decimals
Typing the taxable value instead: Discount% = (1 − Taxable value ÷ (Qty × Rate)) × 100, rounded to 2 decimals; the taxable value is then worked out again from that discount
Example
LineWorkingAmount
24 pouches @ ₹66.67, 5% discount24 × 66.67 × 0.95 = 1520.076₹1,520.08
GST 5%1520.08 × 5%₹76.00 (CGST 38.00 + SGST 38.00)
10 jars @ ₹95, no discount10 × 95₹950.00
GST 5%950 × 5%₹47.50 (CGST 23.75 + SGST 23.75)
InvoiceTaxable 2,470.08 + GST 123.50 = 2,593.58₹2,594 (round off +0.42)
Example
Working backwards: 450 × ₹66.67 = ₹30,001.50. Type taxable value ₹20,000 → discount = (1 − 20,000 ÷ 30,001.50) × 100 = 33.3367% → 33.34%. Taxable value = 30,001.50 × 0.6666 = ₹19,999.00, ₹1.00 below the typed figure; the app shows this gap under the box. GST 5% = ₹999.95, line total ₹20,998.95.
🧮 Try it: one invoice line

Sales: the invoice's place of supply is compared with the business state in Settings. Purchases: the supplier's state is compared. Same state → CGST + SGST; different → IGST.

Example
Business in Tamil Nadu (33). Customer in Kerala (32) → IGST 5%. Customer in Chennai → CGST 2.5% + SGST 2.5%.
Net rate = Rate × (1 − Discount%)
Profit per unit = Net rate − Cost
Markup on cost % = Profit ÷ Cost × 100
Margin on sale % = Profit ÷ Net rate × 100
Typing a markup: Rate = Cost × (1 + Markup%) ÷ (1 − Discount%)
Example
Cost ₹41.26, rate ₹66.66, no discount → profit 25.40; markup 25.40 ÷ 41.26 = 61.56%; margin 25.40 ÷ 66.66 = 38.10%.
Wanting 60% markup with a 5% discount → 41.26 × 1.60 ÷ 0.95 = ₹69.49 rate.
A 50% markup is only a 33.3% margin. Margin can never reach 100%; markup can.
🧮 Try it: markup and margin

A purchased batch is valued at the purchase rate before GST. GST on purchases is input tax credit, recovered in GSTR-3B, so it is not a cost.

Example
100 kg dry ginger @ ₹180 + 5% GST. Batch cost = ₹180 per kg; the ₹900 GST goes to input credit.

Sales and production take stock from the batch that expires first (batches without expiry come last), skipping expired batches. One line can draw from several batches.

Example
Need 20 kg ginger. Batch A has 12 kg (expires March), batch B 50 kg (expires June) → 12 kg from A, 8 kg from B.
Material cost = Σ (quantity taken from each batch × that batch's cost)
Batch cost per unit = Material cost ÷ Quantity made
Lot cost = Material cost + Labour + Electricity (units × rate) + Fuel + Other
Lot cost per unit = Lot cost ÷ Quantity made
Example
ItemWorkingAmount
Ginger12 kg × ₹180 + 8 kg × ₹190₹3,680
Jaggery10 kg × ₹55₹550
Pouches400 × ₹1.20₹480
Materials400 pouches made → 4,710 ÷ 400₹4,710 · ₹11.78 per unit
Labour2 workers × ₹500₹1,000
Electricity12 units × ₹8₹96
Gas₹350
Lot cost6,156 ÷ 400₹6,156 · ₹15.39 per unit

Why the batch is valued at materials only (₹11.78): labour, power and fuel are already booked as expenses on the day. Putting them into stock value as well would count them twice in profit. The lot cost (₹15.39) is shown for pricing decisions.

🧮 Try it: a production run
Run cost of a monthly worker = Monthly salary ÷ 26 (working days)
Example
Salary ₹13,000 → ₹500 for the run. Their salary is still paid and booked once a month through Payroll; the daily overhead removes the part already counted in runs so it is not doubled.
Current cost = Σ (units left × cost) ÷ Σ units left, over good (not expired) batches
If no batch cost is known: the fallback cost in Products
Cost of goods sold = Σ sold quantity × cost at the time of sale − cost of returns put back into stock
Example
Batch 1: 100 left @ ₹11.78; batch 2: 300 left @ ₹12.40 → (1,178 + 3,720) ÷ 400 = ₹12.25. Selling 50 pouches books 50 × 12.25 = ₹612.50 as cost of goods sold.
Day's cost = Materials used + Direct making costs of the day + Overhead share
Direct making costs = run costs (labour, power, fuel, other) + variable expenses under making heads that day
Overhead rate (per unit) = Month's fixed making costs ÷ Units made in the month so far
Overhead share = Units made that day × Overhead rate
Cost per unit = Day's cost ÷ Units made

Fixed making costs = fixed expenses under Procurement, Processing, Labour, Ingredients and Packaging (salaries, electricity bill, machine AMC) + depreciation. Salary already costed in runs is taken out, and the electricity bill is left out when runs record electricity.

Example
